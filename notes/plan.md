# Plan for completing the document together

Work through these steps in order, one at a time. This is the order of doing the work; the final document can keep its current section order. Assess the cases before drafting the client email so the questions and proposed next step are informed by the analysis.

For each step: discuss the options, make the necessary judgment, draft only the relevant part, and review it together before moving on. The user controls what enters `deliverables/document.md`. Use `approach.md` as material to challenge, not as an already agreed answer. Record only confirmed substantive decisions in `../decision-record.md`.

Aim to finish by Monday 12 October 2026; Wednesday 14 October is the absolute final deadline. The steps below are still pending joint review, even where initial brainstorming already exists.

## Sequential steps

1. [ ] **Agree on our reading of the client's request.** Re-read the simulated email and organizational context. Identify the competing priorities, the desired first outcome and the constraints. Separate what is stated from what we would need to ask. Finish when we can explain the problem in our own words without adding unsupported facts. Keep this as working understanding; it does not require an extra section in the deliverable.

2. [ ] **Check what DualOS supports and what remains uncertain.** Read the role guide and technical reference against the kinds of work requested. Distinguish documented capabilities, checks needed in the client environment and substantial additional development. Resolve confusing terms before relying on them. Finish when we can explain the technical boundaries behind our classifications; do not try to obtain platform access or build anything for this exercise.

3. [ ] **Classify all ten cases, one at a time.** For each case, identify the requested outcome, the scope being assessed, the technical class, the reason and the main condition. Split mixed components explicitly. Challenge the initial classifications in `approach.md` rather than copying them automatically. Review each row, then put the agreed rows into the appendix's ten-case table using the required columns. Finish when all ten cases are covered and each classification has a defensible explanation.

4. [ ] **Choose five cases to examine more closely and complete their comparison.** Decide which five give us a useful comparison, including credible candidates and alternatives worth challenging. For each, assess value and how to verify it, compatibility, inputs/dependencies, complexity/uncertainty and relative priority. Use the six required columns and review the rows before adding them to the appendix. Finish when we can compare the candidates without inventing savings, readiness or certainty.

5. [ ] **Choose the first pilot, the next case and what to defer.** Weigh the alternatives using the comparison. Test whether the preferred case remains useful if a key dependency fails, and identify what evidence would change the choice. Check exactly what work the second case could reuse and what would still be new. Record the confirmed judgments and reasons, then compile section 2. Finish when the choices, trade-offs and conditions are clear; a conditional recommendation is acceptable where the exercise lacks decisive information.

6. [ ] **Define the pilot, one field at a time.** Work through the six headings in section 3: result/scope, data/dependencies, necessary work, value/quality, responsibilities, and controls/revision. Decide the useful output and exclusions before discussing implementation. Identify how missing baselines and inputs would be gathered, who reviews results and what would cause a change or stop. Review and compile each field separately. Finish when the fields describe one consistent, bounded pilot.

7. [ ] **Build the roadmap from the pilot's dependencies.** Put the work in a feasible order across six weeks. For each milestone, decide the observable result, owner and condition to continue. Check what happens if access, data or user availability is delayed; adjust scope or timing rather than assuming everything arrives. Then address user verification and possible extension. Compile section 4 once the sequence is coherent and conditional commitments are explicit.

8. [ ] **Write the client email.** Return to the original email and decide who should attend, which information to request and which next step to propose. Reduce the questions to at most five that could materially affect the plan. Explain how DS and FDE would work in terms useful to the client. Draft and review section 1 as one email, with participants and questions inside it. Finish when the reply gives a concrete reason to meet and avoids presenting our assumed checks as completed discovery or the pilot as an unconditional promise.

9. [ ] **Choose and write the two professional episodes.** Identify a real stakeholder-conflict episode and a real technical-obstacle episode from the user's experience. Establish the user's responsibility, actions, outcome and what they would change today; resolve missing facts before drafting. Anonymize where necessary. Compile and review each episode in section 5 separately. Finish when both accounts are accurate and the user's own contribution is clear.

10. [ ] **Review the complete document for consistency and brevity.** Read it from beginning to end. Check that the email, priorities, pilot and roadmap tell the same story; every required field is covered; and claims are supported or marked as assumptions/checks. Challenge the recommendation with a plausible delay or changed priority. Remove duplication and use appendix references where helpful. Agree any substantive revisions before editing the affected sections.

11. [ ] **Prepare the document for delivery.** Add the actual time spent, supplied by the user. Check the appendix schemas and references, then render/export the document to verify the main text fits approximately 4-5 pages with the tables in an appendix if needed. Review the rendered result and make only agreed corrections. Finish when the user has reviewed the complete document and its delivery version.

## After the document is agreed

Use the approved argument to prepare and record the roughly ten-minute client-meeting video. At final packaging, export the assignment sessions raw for direct delivery and check the submission files and video accessibility. These are subsequent tasks; they do not authorize filling unreviewed document sections now.

## Continuing in a later session

Resume at the first unchecked step, or the step the user explicitly selects. Tick a step only after its work has been reviewed together. Read the relevant source material and any accepted substantive decisions before continuing; do not restart the whole analysis or automatically work through the remaining steps.
